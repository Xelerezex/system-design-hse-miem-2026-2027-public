# ADR-101 — Snapshot sector price at hold creation

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-11-13 · **Scope:** HW1 V2

## Context

The price increases by 10% after each 100 **paid** seats in the same `(event_id, sector_id)`. A pending hold must not count as a sale; a price already quoted to the buyer must not change during payment. The expected load is 30 checkouts/s for 60 s, with 40% of purchases targeting one sector.

## Options (CBAM-lite)

Scores are design estimates (1–5), not benchmark results. Lower cost and higher benefit are preferable.

| Factor | A: COUNT confirmed holds | B: Transactional counter |
|---|---:|---:|
| Implementation cost | 2 | 4 |
| Operational cost | 2 | 3 |
| Consistency risk | 2 | 4 |
| Correctness benefit | 5 | 5 |
| Expected scaling benefit | 2 | 5 |
| Observability benefit | 5 | 4 |
| Benefit − cost | **6** | **3** |

## Decision

Start with **A** to establish correctness and a measurable baseline. `catalog` owns `sector_prices`; `booking` owns holds, orders and the sold count. Preserve `price_minor` and `sold_count_at_hold` in each hold. Derive order amounts from those stored prices. Serialize hold pricing and paid confirmation using per-sector PostgreSQL transaction-level advisory locks.

Experiment **B** after profiling: `booking_sector_sales` stores a materialized count. The first successful payment increments it in the same transaction that confirms the holds. Repeat payments must not increment it. Validate with invariant **I8** against the confirmed holds. Keep **I7** for order/hold prices.

## Consequences

- A avoids extra mutable state, but its `COUNT(*)` cost grows with paid rows.
- A partial confirmed-holds index lowers SQL execution time but made HTTP p99 worse in three runs; it is not an established end-to-end improvement.
- B lowers read costs, but adds write work and a new consistency invariant. Its measured HTTP improvement is small.
- Removing sector locks without a replacement consistency mechanism is rejected: it could misprice concurrent holds at a step boundary.
